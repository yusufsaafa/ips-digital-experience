# Information Architecture Proposal

This proposal is for the IPS Digital Experience and should guide future application/page planning. It is intentionally content-first and does not prescribe implementation.

Strategic recommendation: the site should lead with the critical problems IPS helps solve, not with an explanation of IPS as a corporate platform. Corporate structure becomes useful after a visitor has recognized their problem, found a relevant capability, seen proof, and needs the right specialist.

## Narrative Model

The recommended site narrative is:

1. Problem.
2. Solution capability.
3. Proof.
4. Relevant IPS specialists.
5. Contact.

This model reflects how technical buyers evaluate high-risk material and component suppliers. They first need to know whether IPS understands their failure mode or operating environment. Only after that do they need to understand which IPS company, product family, or contact path is most relevant.

## Preserved Entry Paths

Visitors should be able to navigate by:

- Problem: mission-critical sealing, EMI/RFI shielding, fire and thermal protection, corrosion and chemical resistance, high-purity processing, harsh-environment material performance.
- Industry: aerospace, defense, medical devices, semiconductor, energy, oil and gas, advanced industrial, commercial electronics.
- Capability: engineered elastomers, sealing, shielding, RF/microwave absorbing materials, fire/thermal protection, molding, lining, additive manufacturing, precision rollers.
- Product: seals, gaskets, O-rings, hoses, bellows, shielding gaskets, conductive fabrics, high-purity vessels, corrosion-resistant linings, rollers.
- Known IPS specialist: Rubbercraft, MAST Technologies, Spira Manufacturing, IRP Medical, RMB Products, and the rest of the IPS portfolio.

## IA Principles

- Lead with the visitor's technical problem before explaining IPS's organization.
- Let users self-select by the language they already have: problem, market, capability, product, or known business.
- Keep the IPS Specialist Network visible, but do not require visitors to understand the portfolio before moving forward.
- Put proof near every conversion point.
- Avoid generic advanced-materials claims unless paired with specific applications, problems, and product families.
- Route complex inquiries through a single IPS-level intake path with optional specialist selection.
- Clearly label strategic recommendations as proposals until validated by IPS stakeholders.
