# Website Content Hierarchy

This hierarchy translates the research into a content model for the future IPS website. It is a strategic proposal, not a verified description of the current IPS website.

## Home Page Hierarchy

### 1. Hero

Recommended content:

- Concrete promise around solving high-stakes material and component problems.
- Problem language such as mission-critical sealing, shielding, fire/thermal protection, corrosion resistance, high-purity processing, and harsh-environment performance.
- Primary CTA: Talk to an engineer.
- Secondary CTA: Explore problems we solve.

Avoid using "Advanced Materials Platform" as the main hero message unless it is attached to a concrete problem-oriented promise.

Visitor goal supported: Quickly determine whether IPS understands the kind of critical technical problem they need solved.

IPS business goal supported: Attract qualified technical buyers and prevent early exits caused by vague corporate positioning.

Why this appears first: Visitors arrive with urgent operating problems, not a desire to decode the company structure.

### 2. Problems We Solve

Recommended content:

- Mission-critical sealing.
- EMI/RFI shielding.
- Fire and thermal protection.
- Corrosion and chemical resistance.
- High-purity processing.
- Harsh-environment material performance.

Each problem should link to relevant capabilities, industries, product families, proof, and contact paths.

Visitor goal supported: Find a recognizable problem statement before choosing an industry, product, or business unit.

IPS business goal supported: Route visitors into high-value solution areas and clarify the breadth of IPS expertise.

Why this appears second: It immediately translates the hero promise into actionable paths.

### 3. Industries

Recommended content:

- Aerospace.
- Defense.
- Medical Devices.
- Semiconductor.
- Energy And Industrial.
- Space, oil and gas, advanced industrial, and commercial electronics where validated.

Visitor goal supported: Confirm relevance to the buyer's operating environment and regulatory/commercial context.

IPS business goal supported: Segment demand by market so inquiries can be routed, qualified, and messaged appropriately.

Why this appears third: Once visitors recognize the problem, industry context helps them trust that IPS understands their environment.

### 4. Capabilities

Recommended content:

- Engineered elastomers.
- Sealing and gaskets.
- EMI/RFI shielding.
- RF and microwave absorbing materials.
- Fire and thermal protection.
- Medical silicone and rubber molding.
- Rotational molding and lining.
- Corrosion protection.
- Composite fabrication enabling.
- Additive manufacturing.
- Precision rollers.

Visitor goal supported: Understand the technical ways IPS can solve the identified problem.

IPS business goal supported: Shift the conversation from general interest to solution-fit and technical qualification.

Why this appears fourth: Capabilities make sense after the visitor has seen problem and industry relevance.

### 5. Engineering Process

Recommended content:

- Discover: clarify operating environment, failure mode, requirements, constraints, and program stage.
- Engineer: select materials, refine geometry, evaluate manufacturability, and define the solution approach.
- Prototype: build samples, tooling paths, or early production-intent components where appropriate.
- Validate / Qualify: test against requirements, documentation needs, quality expectations, and customer qualification gates.
- Manufacture: scale into repeatable production with the relevant IPS specialist.
- Support: sustain production, lifecycle needs, aftermarket/MRO needs, and future design iterations.

Visitor goal supported: Understand how IPS turns a problem into a qualified manufactured solution.

IPS business goal supported: Build confidence with technical buyers, increase inquiry quality, and set expectations for collaboration.

Why this appears fifth: After problem, industry, and capability fit are established, process proof reduces perceived execution risk.

### 6. IPS Specialist Network / Portfolio

Recommended framing:

"One IPS platform, multiple specialized engineering companies."

Recommended content:

- Specialist cards organized by problem/capability rather than alphabetical corporate listing.
- Clear descriptions of each company's focus.
- Link to each business website or contact path.
- CTA: Find the right IPS specialist.

Visitor goal supported: Access deep specialist expertise without needing to understand IPS's corporate structure first.

IPS business goal supported: Preserve brand equity across specialist companies while presenting IPS as a coherent route to solutions.

Why this appears sixth: The portfolio becomes most useful once the visitor knows what problem and capability they care about.

### 7. Quality, Compliance And Proof

Recommended content:

- Certifications and registrations, verified by business unit before publication.
- Defense and regulated-market readiness where applicable and verified.
- Engineering and production quality systems.
- Application examples or case studies where available and approved.
- Testing, qualification, and documentation practices where validated.

Visitor goal supported: Reduce supplier risk and gather evidence for internal technical/procurement review.

IPS business goal supported: Improve credibility, shorten supplier evaluation, and support regulated-market conversion.

Why this appears seventh: Proof lands strongest after the visitor has already identified relevance and the likely solution path.

### 8. Contact CTA

Recommended content:

- Talk to an engineer.
- Request a quote.
- Submit a drawing or specification.
- Find the right IPS specialist.

Visitor goal supported: Take the next step with the right technical context.

IPS business goal supported: Convert qualified visitors into routeable inquiries with enough detail for follow-up.

Why this appears last: The full page has prepared the visitor to submit a more specific, higher-quality inquiry.

## Industry Page Hierarchy

1. Industry-specific problem promise.
2. Typical operating environments and failure modes.
3. Problems IPS helps solve in that industry.
4. Relevant capabilities.
5. Relevant product families.
6. Relevant IPS specialists.
7. Proof points and certifications, verified by business unit.
8. Application examples where approved.
9. CTA.

## Problem Page Hierarchy

1. Problem definition.
2. Why the problem matters in critical environments.
3. Relevant solution capabilities.
4. Related product families.
5. Industries affected.
6. Relevant IPS specialists.
7. Proof, quality, and compliance considerations.
8. Engineering process connection.
9. CTA.

## Capability Page Hierarchy

1. Capability definition.
2. Problems solved.
3. Materials and technologies.
4. Products.
5. Industries served.
6. Relevant IPS specialists.
7. Quality/compliance proof, verified by business unit.
8. Engineering process.
9. CTA.

## Product Page Hierarchy

1. Product family overview.
2. Problems addressed.
3. Applications.
4. Materials and performance considerations.
5. Customization options.
6. Related capabilities.
7. Related industries.
8. Relevant IPS specialists.
9. Specification/drawing CTA.

## IPS Specialist Page Hierarchy

1. Specialist identity and engineering focus.
2. Problems addressed.
3. Markets served.
4. Core capabilities.
5. Product families.
6. Certifications/facility proof, verified before publication.
7. Relationship to the IPS specialist network.
8. Visit business website/contact CTA.

## Contact Page Hierarchy

1. Choose action: talk to an engineer, request quote, submit drawing/spec, find the right IPS specialist.
2. Industry.
3. Problem or application challenge.
4. Capability or product interest.
5. Program phase.
6. Compliance/certification needs.
7. Upload drawing/specification.
8. Preferred IPS specialist, if known.
9. Contact details.

## Claims And Verification Rule

All certifications, compliance claims, facility descriptions, performance figures, customer examples, and market leadership statements should be reviewed with IPS stakeholders before publication. Strategic content structure in this document is a proposal.
