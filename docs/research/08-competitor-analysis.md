# Competitor Analysis

## Competitive Set

IPS competes across several overlapping categories rather than one neat market:

- Aerospace and defense seals.
- Engineered elastomeric components.
- EMI/RFI shielding.
- Advanced materials and composites.
- Medical elastomeric molding.
- Semiconductor/high-purity polymer systems.
- Fire, thermal, pressure, and corrosion protection.

## Representative Competitors

| Competitor | Relevant Position | Competitive Strength | IPS Opportunity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Greene Tweed | High-performance sealing, thermoplastics, composites, and engineered components for harsh environments | Strong brand equity, global reach, aerospace/defense/semiconductor credibility | Present IPS as a broader portfolio with multiple specialist brands and direct challenge-based routing |
| Trelleborg Sealing Solutions | Global supplier of aerospace sealing solutions and aircraft seals | Scale, broad product catalog, long aerospace history | Emphasize bespoke engineering, advanced material breadth, and specialist business-unit expertise |

| Saint-Gobain Life Sciences / Seals | High-performance polymer, silicone, sealing, and process-system solutions | Global manufacturing, life sciences and industrial credibility | Highlight IPS's aerospace-defense-medical-specialty industrial blend and critical-to-function engineering |
| Freudenberg Sealing Technologies | Broad sealing technology and elastomeric product portfolio | Global scale and mature catalog | Focus IPS around specialized advanced-material problems, speed to engineered solution, and portfolio specialization |

## Strategic Differentiators For IPS

- Portfolio architecture: IPS can credibly organize many specialist brands under one advanced-materials story.
- Breadth across harsh-environment challenges: sealing, shielding, fire, thermal, corrosion, pressure, high purity, and advanced manufacturing.
- Defense and aerospace depth: multiple brands map directly to mission-critical aerospace/defense needs.
- Custom engineering: public sources repeatedly describe custom, bespoke, engineered, and critical-to-function solutions.
- Specialist brand trust: customers may know individual businesses; the digital experience should transfer that trust to IPS.

## Digital Risks

- The portfolio can feel fragmented if the website only lists brands.
- General "advanced materials" language can sound generic without proof and concrete applications.
- Buyers may leave for a known competitor if they cannot quickly identify the right IPS capability.
- Certification and compliance claims need careful business-unit-level validation.

## Content Opportunities

- Build a "Find Your Solution" path by industry, challenge, and product.
- Use brand cards as proof, not the entire IA.
- Create competitor-grade product/capability pages for sealing, shielding, thermal/fire, medical elastomers, and rotational lining.
- Add comparison-friendly proof modules: certifications, facilities, engineering process, materials, application examples.
